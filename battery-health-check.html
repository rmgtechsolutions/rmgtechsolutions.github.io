How to check laptop battery health across your office laptops

RMG Tech SolutionsUpdated 2026

Battery problems show up at the worst time: in a client meeting, at a site visit, in the middle of a presentation. Windows has a built-in report that tells you the truth in about a minute, with nothing to download. Use it on your team's laptops, and on any refurbished laptop before you buy it.

Sound familiar?

  • Does your sales team carry chargers everywhere because laptops die within two hours?
  • Has a laptop shut down in the middle of a client presentation?
  • Did a refurbished laptop sold as "battery like new" turn out otherwise?

A weak battery is a small cost that causes big embarrassment. Check every laptop once, then plan replacements.

Run the report

On the laptop you want to check:

  1. Press the Windows key, type cmd, right-click Command Prompt and choose "Run as administrator".
  2. Type exactly: powercfg /batteryreport and press Enter.
  3. It prints a file path, usually like C:\Users\Name\battery-report.html.
  4. Open that file in any browser.

It takes less than a minute and changes nothing on the machine. It only reads what Windows has already been recording.

The two numbers that matter

Scroll to the section called "Installed batteries". You want these two rows:

Battery report — the key rowsDESIGN CAPACITY ......... what the battery held when new FULL CHARGE CAPACITY .... what it can hold today HEALTH % = Full Charge ÷ Design × 100

For comparison, a battery showing a Design Capacity of 45,000 mWh and a Full Charge Capacity of 27,900 mWh is at 62% health. It is still usable at a desk, but it is time to plan its replacement.

How to read the percentage

  • 90%+ — barely used. Rare on an older laptop; if the machine has many power-on hours, the battery may have been replaced, which is fine but worth knowing.
  • 75–90% — normal for a three-year-old business laptop. No action needed.
  • 60–75% — noticeably shorter backup. Fine for desk users who work plugged in.
  • Below 60% — near the end. Schedule a replacement, starting with staff who travel.

Also check: cycle count and recent history

Further down, the report shows the "Cycle Count" (how many full charge cycles the battery has done). Under 500 is comfortable; over 1,000 means heavy use. Some manufacturers do not report it, so a blank field is not a red flag by itself.

The "Battery capacity history" section shows the last few months. If capacity has dropped sharply recently, the battery is degrading fast right now, which is worse than a low but stable number.

Plan replacements, don't react to them

For a team, run the report on every laptop once (or let us do it during an AMC visit), list the laptops by battery health, and replace those below 60% first, starting with the people who meet clients or work on site. A genuine manufacturer battery costs more than a compatible one but usually lasts longer; we quote both after checking the exact model. Replacement takes minutes on models with an external battery and longer on sealed ones.

Safety first: if a laptop's touchpad is lifting, the case is bulging or the lid no longer closes flat, the battery may be swelling. Stop using that laptop and get the battery replaced immediately. A swollen battery is a fire risk.

Buying refurbished? One non-negotiable

If a seller refuses to let you run this report, walk away. It is a one-minute, read-only command. A low battery on an otherwise excellent laptop is not a deal-breaker — just include the replacement in the price.

Common questions

When should a laptop battery be replaced?

When its health falls below about 60% of design capacity, when backup no longer covers the user's working pattern, or immediately if the battery is swelling.

Can you replace batteries for our office laptops?

Yes. We check the exact model, quote for a compatible or a genuine battery, and fit it on site.
