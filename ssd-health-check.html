Checks

How to check SSD health on your office computers (before you lose data)

RMG Tech SolutionsUpdated 2026

Of all the parts in a computer, the SSD hides its condition best. A worn drive still starts Windows today, and then takes your accounts data, invoices and client files with it when it fails. This is the check we run on every machine we service or supply. It takes five minutes.

Sound familiar?

  • Is your company's Tally data on one PC, with no backup anywhere else?
  • Has a computer suddenly stopped starting, taking weeks of work with it?
  • Do you know the health of the drives in your office PCs today?

A failed drive is not just a hardware cost. It is lost invoices, lost records and days of work redone. Checking takes five minutes; recovery can take weeks, if it is possible at all.

Step 1: Install CrystalDiskInfo

CrystalDiskInfo is free, small and the standard tool for this check. Download it from the official site (crystalmark.info), install it and open it. The portable version runs from a pen drive without installation, which is useful for checking several PCs or a refurbished laptop before you buy it. On company PCs you may need administrator rights.

Step 2: Read the three numbers that matter

CrystalDiskInfo — what to readHEALTH STATUS ....... Good (blue) = fine Caution (yellow) = back up now, plan replacement Bad (red) = stop, back up immediately HEALTH PERCENT ...... 90%+ excellent · 80–90% acceptable below 70% = plan replacement POWER-ON HOURS ...... under 10,000 hrs = light office use 20,000+ hrs = ran day and night

Health Status is the summary verdict. "Good" in blue means the drive's internal monitoring (called SMART data) shows no serious wear. "Caution" usually means some memory cells have failed or spare blocks are being used up. It might run another year, or another week. Nobody can promise which.

The percentage is the remaining rated life. SSDs wear out by writing — every memory cell can only be written a limited number of times. A drive at 95% has barely been used; a drive at 65% has spent years in heavy use and should be replaced on a planned date.

Power-on hours tell the machine's real life story. On refurbished laptops this number often contradicts the description in the listing. Trust the data.

Step 3: A quick speed check

A drive can be healthy and still slow — some sellers fit the cheapest possible SSD before selling. Run CrystalDiskMark (same developer, also free) once:

  • SATA SSD: expect roughly 400–550 MB/s read. Fine for office use.
  • NVMe SSD: expect 1,500 MB/s or more. If an "NVMe" drive shows 400 MB/s, something is wrong.
  • Under 200 MB/s read: either a failing drive or a hard disk presented as an SSD. Investigate.

What to do with the result

  • Good, 80% or more: no action. Check again at the next maintenance visit.
  • Caution, or below 70%: back up now and schedule a replacement. Copy the data to the new drive before the old one fails.
  • Bad: stop using the machine for important work and back up immediately.
  • Buying refurbished: a weak SSD is a negotiation point, not always a deal-breaker, because drives are easy to replace. Walk away if the status is "Bad" or the seller refuses to let you run the check.

Make it routine, not an emergency

For offices, we include drive health checks in our AMC maintenance visits, with a record for every machine, so a failing drive is replaced on a planned day — not discovered on the morning the PC will not start.

And remember: a health check tells you a drive is wearing out; only a backup protects your data. Keep at least one copy of business data away from the PC itself, on an external drive, a NAS or the cloud, and test that it actually restores.

Common questions

How often should office computers be checked?

At every maintenance visit, and at least every three to six months. Under our AMC, drive health is checked and recorded for every machine.

Does a good SSD health result mean our data is safe?

No. It tells you the drive is not wearing out, but drives can still fail suddenly. Only a separate, tested backup protects business data.
