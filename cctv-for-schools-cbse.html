CCTV for schools: what CBSE board-exam centres need before February

RMG Tech SolutionsUpdated 2026

Since the 2025 board exams, CBSE has made CCTV a condition for any school that wants to be a Class 10 or 12 examination centre. A school without working CCTV is simply not made a centre. With the next board exams starting in February, the weeks before are when every camera, recorder and hard disk should be checked.

Sound familiar?

  • Do you know which classroom cameras are working today, and which only show a black screen?
  • Is your recorder actually saving footage, or did its hard disk fail months ago?
  • Can you produce exam-day footage two months after the results if the board asks for it?

A dead camera found in February is a problem; a dead camera found in November is a quick fix.

What the CBSE policy asks for

  • Coverage of the whole examination hall — entrances, exits and the examination desks.
  • Recording, not just live view — the footage must be stored and reviewable.
  • Keeping the footage — for the 2025 exams, recordings had to be kept for two months after the results were declared.
  • Notices and trained staff — students and staff are told about the cameras, notices are displayed, and the staff who operate and review the system are trained.
  • The school's own cost — schools that want to be centres install CCTV themselves.

The policy does not fix a camera model or resolution. What matters is that every exam room is clearly covered, every camera records, and the footage can be produced when asked. Always read the latest CBSE circular for the current exam year.

The pre-exam CCTV checklist

  1. Every exam room camera works — open each camera on the recorder and look for black screens, blurred or misaligned views.
  2. The view covers all desks, the entrance and the exit — a camera that sees only half the room is a gap.
  3. The recorder is recording — play back yesterday's footage from every camera, not just the live view.
  4. The hard disk is healthy — check its status in the recorder; a disk showing errors or "unformatted" saves nothing.
  5. Date and time are correct — wrong timestamps make footage useless as a record.
  6. Night vision and lighting — early-morning or dim halls need IR working.
  7. Power backup — the recorder and the PoE switch on a UPS, so a power cut does not stop recording mid-exam.
  8. Notices displayed at the centre, as the policy requires.

The storage trap most schools miss

A typical recorder keeps 15 to 30 days of footage and then overwrites the oldest. Board exams run for weeks, and the footage must survive until two months after the results — several months in total. The recorder alone cannot do that.

The fix is simple: after each exam day, export that day's footage from the exam-room cameras to a separate drive, label it by date and room, and store it safely. See our CCTV storage guide to work out how many days your recorder actually keeps.

Beyond the exams

The same system protects students every day: gates, corridors, stairs, the school bus bay and the computer lab. Parents increasingly ask about it during admissions. Once the exam rooms are covered, plan the rest of the campus in one go rather than camera by camera.

Common questions

Is CCTV mandatory for CBSE board exam centres?

Yes. Under CBSE's CCTV policy, introduced for the 2025 board exams, a school without CCTV facilities is not considered as an examination centre. Check the latest CBSE circular for each exam year.

How long must exam CCTV footage be kept?

For the 2025 exams CBSE asked schools to keep the recordings for two months after the results were declared. Most recorders overwrite footage after 15 to 30 days, so exam-day footage should be exported to a separate drive.
